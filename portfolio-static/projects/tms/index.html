TMS

TMS Transport Management System

TMS
Enterprise ProductTMSInteraction Design

Project Background

We built a transport platform for a large steel group, replacing paper waybills and phone dispatch with digital work orders and system-based dispatch. As operations grew, complex workflows made everyday tasks inefficient.

Phase two focused on cost-effective, end-to-end control: reducing cognitive load and simplifying operations before, during and after each trip.

How Users Decide & Act

We spent a week in the dispatch centre, shadowing dispatchers across shifts, conducting in-depth interviews and observing work on site. We also interviewed several external carriers and analysed comparable logistics platforms.

Research and a product review revealed three core issues: fragmented information, difficult decision-making and limited process visibility.

Project Contracts: Overview & Hierarchy

Previously, finding expiring contracts took several filtering steps, with no overview of what needed attention. Key information and contract statuses were hard to identify.

Inquiry Centre: Simpler Quoting

Users struggled with filters such as “internal” and “external” orders and had too many fields to complete. Quote cards lacked key information, forcing users into detail pages before they could make a pricing decision.

Vehicle Monitoring: Clear Context & Records

Previously, checking vehicle exceptions meant switching between the map and detail pages, taking two minutes per alert. Some dispatchers used sticky notes on their screens to track vehicles that needed attention.

Project Reflection

This project reinforced that enterprise UX should help people decide what to focus on and what to do next. A clear attention hierarchy gives visual design a purpose: supporting more efficient work.
